# Copernicus Earth Observation Data for Wildfire Risk Assessment and Management

## Published Research

**Author:** Alessio Tafuni

**Co-authors:** Crescenza Calculli, Alessio Pollice

**Publication:** Metodi e Analisi Statistiche (MAS) 2024

---

## Overview

This research analyses the spatial distribution and environmental impact of wildfires in Italy during the 2023 summer season using Earth Observation data provided by the European Union Copernicus Programme.

The study focuses on wildfire risk assessment, active fire monitoring and burnt area analysis through geospatial and remote sensing techniques.

The work was subsequently published in *Metodi e Analisi Statistiche (MAS) 2024*.

---

## Research Objectives

- Assess wildfire risk across Italy during summer 2023
- Monitor active fires using satellite observations
- Analyse the spatial distribution of burned areas
- Evaluate vegetation types most affected by wildfires
- Support environmental monitoring and disaster management activities

---

## Data Sources

### Copernicus Emergency Management Service (CEMS)

The study uses datasets provided by:

- Copernicus Emergency Management Service (CEMS)
- European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS)
- Climate Data Store (CDS)

The analysis combines both raster and vector geospatial datasets.

## Main Results

### Wildfire Risk

The highest wildfire risk levels were concentrated in Southern Italy and the major islands, particularly during July and August 2023.

### Active Fires

Fire Radiative Power (FRP) revealed a strong concentration of high-intensity fires in Southern Italy.

K-Means clustering identified a specific group of high-intensity wildfires (megafires) characterised by higher thermal intensity and vegetation density.

### Burned Areas

The most affected regions were:

- Sicily
- Calabria

The largest wildfire recorded during the study exceeded 3,100 burned hectares.

### Vegetation Impact

The most affected ecosystems included:

- Mediterranean shrubland
- Sclerophyll forests
- Broadleaf forests

Burned areas exceeded 35,000 hectares in some island territories.
